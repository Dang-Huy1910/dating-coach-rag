# Writing a dating bio / profile

## What a bio is for

A short bio should tell the reader three things: who you are in a concrete way, what you like to do, and the kind of connection you are looking for. Avoid generic slogans (“live life to the fullest”, “love life”) — they give the other person nothing to open with.

## Length

A good bio is usually 300–600 characters or 3–5 lines. Enough for a conversation hook, not a résumé. If the app is very short, one specific detail beats a pile of adjectives.

## Specific beats “nice”

“Weekend flower-market runs and homemade phở” is stronger than “I like food”. “Comics and a 5 km run” is stronger than “I love sports”. Small details are opener fuel for whoever reads you.

## Tone

Write like you talk — not a job CV, not a forced flirty caption. Light humor is fine if it does not punch down. You do not need to flex income or list requirements like a hiring brief.

## What to avoid

- Only emoji, or a single “message me and you’ll see”
- A long “I don’t like people who…” list
- Vague promises (“looking for my other half”, “my perfect match”)
- Sensitive details you do not need (home address, phone number)

## A simple structure

1. One line about work or daily rhythm (no flashy title required)
2. One line about a hobby you could actually do on a date
3. One line about what you want on the app (friends, something serious, coffee) — said plainly, not as pressure
