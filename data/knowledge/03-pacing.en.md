# Conversation pacing

## Don’t stack messages

After an opener, wait for a reply. Sending three more texts while they have not answered creates pressure. One clear message, then stop, is enough.

## Match length

If they reply short, don’t answer with a novel. If they ask a question, answer it before adding extra. Stay close to their rhythm; don’t “fix” them into yours.

## Moving from chat to a meetup

After 2–3 exchanges with real content (not just emoji), you can suggest a public place, a specific time, and an easy out. “Coffee 30 minutes near Cầu Giấy this weekend?” is easier than “Let’s hang out.”

## When they reply slowly

Slow is not automatically disrespect. Don’t guilt-trip (“you must hate me”). One light ping after a few days is fine, then let them choose.

## Signs to ease off or stop

One-word replies forever, topic changes when you mention meeting, or “busy season” — lower frequency. Pushing for a date makes the chat heavy.

## Leave an exit

Every invite should let them say no without a long explanation. “No worries if you’re not free” is healthy pacing, not weakness.
