# Boundaries and consent (not clinical)

## Saying it early is allowed

You can name the pace you want: slow chat, daytime meetups, no video yet. State it as information, not an order. “I prefer a busy public place the first time” is a clear boundary.

## Consent in messages

Don’t push intimate topics they have not opened. If they change the subject or reply short, drop it. “No” or silence is not an invitation to try harder.

## This is not therapy

These notes do not diagnose trauma and do not replace a professional. If a conversation scares you or feels controlling, seek a real person you trust — the coach is not a clinician.

## A polite no

“I’m not up for that kind of meetup; daytime coffee only” refuses and states a condition. You don’t need an over-the-top apology. If they don’t agree, stop — don’t negotiate the boundary away.

## Photos and personal data

Don’t demand private photos, a home address, or ID. Don’t send what they didn’t ask for. Dating apps are not a place to harvest a stranger’s data.

## When a boundary is ignored

If you said no and they repeat the same ask, that is a stop signal, not a challenge to “teach” them.
