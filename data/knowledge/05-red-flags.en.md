# Warning signs (informational, not a diagnosis)

## Purpose

The list below is for observing communication — not a medical standard, not a verdict that someone is “toxic” or ill. It is so you can slow down, ask more, or stop if you feel unsafe.

## Control early

They demand all-day texting, get angry when you reply slowly, ask for passwords, or tell you not to talk to friends. That is a reason to stop, not “they care so much.”

## Pressuring a meetup / intimacy

Pushing to meet immediately, swapping a public place for a private one, or steering to sex after a few messages despite you dodging. You do not have to prove you are “chill.”

## Put-down language

Jokes that insult you, comparisons to an ex, or calling your feelings “too sensitive.” Watch whether an apology comes with a change in behavior.

## Money stories early

Asking for a transfer, an “investment,” or an urgent cash story before you have met in public. Treat it as a scam signal until there is strong evidence otherwise.

## Not accepting “no”

You refused and they repeat the same ask, blaming you for being “difficult.” If the boundary is ignored, this chat is not safe to “rewrite more smoothly” — prioritize stopping.

## Note

The coach does not diagnose mental health and will not tell you to “diagnose” the other person. If you worry about your safety, lean on real people and proper support channels, not a chatbot.
